Prompt:
You are an Automated Fraud Forensic Engine. You analyze transaction records to 
determine fraud probability scores. REASONING DIRECTIVE: You must employ a Controlled 
Step-by-Step Verification Protocol. Do NOT construct narrative backstories or 
hypothetical explanations (e.g., "The cardholder was likely traveling and lost their 
wallet"). Evaluate transactions solely against mathematical anomalies, physical 
impossibility thresholds, and velocity limits. EXECUTE THE FOLLOWING CONTROLLED 
EVALUATION PHASES: Phase 1: Anomaly Extraction (Rule Verification) Evaluate the 
transaction record against these explicit checks: - Velocity Check: >3 transactions in 
<10 minutes? - Geolocation Velocity: Physical movement required between consecutive 
transactions >900 km/h? - Deviation from Baseline: Single transaction amount >400% of 
historical average? - Foreign Terminal Flag: Transaction originating outside home 
country without prior travel notification? Phase 2: Tabular Evidence Synthesis List 
only failed rules in a verification table: | Rule Tested | Calculated Metric from Data 
| Threshold | Status (PASS / BREACH) | Risk Point Contribution | |---|---|---|---|---| 
Phase 3: Controlled Risk Calculation Apply this scoring index to calculate total risk: - Geolocation Impossibility = 45 pts - Velocity Breach = 25 pts - Extreme Deviation 
(>400%) = 20 pts - Terminal Risk = 10 pts Total Risk Score = Sum of points (Scale 0 to 
100). Phase 4: Structured Final Verdict Provide a deterministic JSON output block: 
```json { "total_risk_score": <int 0-100>, "risk_tier": "<LOW (0-30) | MEDIUM (31-69) | 
HIGH (70-100)>", "primary_triggers": ["<breached rule 1>", "<breached rule 2>"], 
"recommended_action": "<APPROVE | STEP_UP_AUTH | FREEZE_ACCOUNT>", "confidence_level": 
"<LOW | MODERATE | HIGH>" } ``` <transaction_data> {{TRANSACTION_SUMMARY_TEXT}} 
</transaction_data>

Method Decision: Controlled Reasoning is strictly selected over Chain of Thought (CoT) and Tree of
Thought (ToT).

Why not open Chain of Thought? Unconstrained CoT triggers narrative confabulation in fraud analysis. When
told to "think step by step," LLMs invent plausible backstories (e.g., "The user may have made an emergency
airport purchase") which inappropriately downrates legitimate fraud flags.

Why not Tree of Thought? ToT branches into speculative hypothetical pathways. In high-throughput
operational fraud detection, branching introduces unpredictable latency, high compute overhead, and
subjective exploration where deterministic rule-checks are required.

Why Controlled Reasoning? Enforces linear progression through deterministic mathematical and rule-based
verification gates, bounding scores strictly between 0 and 100.

ALTERNATIVE PROMPT
Execute a strict programmatic rule-check over the transaction payload in <payload>. For 
each check (Time Delta, Geo Delta, Amount Delta), calculate the exact numeric 
difference. If Geo Delta divided by Time Delta exceeds 900 km/h, increment fraud score 
by 50. If amount > 4x average, increment by 30. Output only the calculated numbers and 
the final score integer. No natural language justification permitted.

Q7. Classification with Overlapping Edge Cases

ZERO-SHOT PROMPT DESIGN:
You are a Customer Service Triage Classifier. Classify the customer complaint provided 
below into exactly ONE of the following categories: - Billing - Network - Device - 
Other DEFINITIONS: - Billing: Inquiries or disputes regarding charges, fees, invoices, 
plan costs, overages, or payments. - Network: Physical signal issues, call drops, data 
speed degradations, cell tower outages, or coverage gaps. - Device: Physical hardware 
malfunction, firmware glitches, SIM card read errors, or handset operational issues. - 
Other: Inquiries not mapping cleanly to the above (e.g., general store hours, brand 
sponsorship complaints). CLASSIFICATION RULES: - If a complaint mentions multiple 
categories, identify the PRIMARY ROOT CAUSE. - Return only valid JSON: {"category": "
<Billing | Network | Device | Other>", "confidence": <float 0-1>, "reason": "<one 
sentence justification>"} Complaint: "My phone screen keeps flickering and showing a 
'No SIM' alert every time I try to check my mobile data bill, and yesterday my 5G 
dropped entirely."

FEW-SHOT PROMPT DESIGN:
You are a Customer Service Triage Classifier. Classify incoming customer complaints 
into exactly ONE category: [Billing, Network, Device, Other]. When multiple issues are 
present, classify by ROOT CAUSE rather than secondary symptoms. --- EXAMPLES --- Input: 
"I have no internet, and because of that, I couldn't log into the app to pay my monthly 
invoice on time." Reasoning: The root failure is signal/connectivity loss; the 
inability to pay the invoice is an operational consequence. Classification: Network 
Input: "My screen is cracked and now the phone keeps restarting whenever the 5G radio 
connects." Reasoning: Handset physical damage is triggering the operational failure, 
despite mention of the 5G network. Classification: Device Input: "I got hit with a $50 
roaming charge because my phone accidentally toggled roaming on when the network was 
weak." Reasoning: The core dispute is over an unexpected charge on the statement, 
making it a financial/account grievance. Classification: Billing Input: "I went into 
the retail branch to ask why my SIM card tray was jammed, and the sales representative 
was rude to me." Reasoning: The primary complaint centers on staff behavior and retail 
experience, which is outside hardware, carrier signal, or accounts. Classification: 
Other Input: "The carrier software update pushed to my phone yesterday caused my 
battery to drain in two hours and corrupted my Wi-Fi calling." Reasoning: Carrier
pushed firmware/operating system instability affecting hardware operation maps to 
Device. Classification: Device --- END OF EXAMPLES --- Classify the following 
complaint: Input: "My phone screen keeps flickering and showing a 'No SIM' alert every 
time I try to check my mobile data bill, and yesterday my 5G dropped entirely." 
Reasoning: [Analyze the root cause vs. secondary symptoms] Classification: [Billing | 
Network | Device | Other]

Why Few-Shot Helps Here:
1. Disambiguates Boundary Precedence: Edge cases
frequently span multiple categories (e.g., Device +
Billing + Network). Few-shot examples teach the
prioritization rule ("classify by root cause rather than
consequence") directly through demonstrations.

2. Calibrates Semantic Thresholds: Demonstrates
exactly how borderline issues (like SIM tray jams or
firmware update battery drain) are categorized without
needing lengthy abstract rules.

3. Standardizes Explanation Syntax: Enforces a
consistent, one-line causal reasoning format before
generating the final label, keeping classifications
consistent across high ticket volumes.

When Few-Shot Breaks:

1. Spurious Keyword Correlation: If all demonstration
examples for "Device" happen to mention "app" or
"screen", the model may develop an overfit heuristic,
latching onto keywords rather than understanding the
underlying context.

2. Label Bias & Prior Skew: If the few-shot examples do
not have an even category balance (e.g., 3 Device
examples and 0 Network examples), the model's
output probabilities become skewed toward the
overrepresented class on ambiguous inputs.

3. Token Saturation & Cost: Adding 5–8 multi-sentence
examples increases prompt token count, significantly
increasing latency and API costs on high-volume
production ticketing pipelines.

Justify] - Edge Case Resilience: Did the response miss obvious boundary failures?

Why this structure: Dividing the generation process into three distinct, non-recursive steps (Draft, Critique,

single, cost-effective inference pass.

Failure modes prevented: Infinite recursive loops, conversational meta-chatter in the final output, and
superficial self-praise (prevented by the four binary pass/fail checks).

PIPELINE STAGE DESIGNS

Stage 1: Ingestion & Schema-Bound Extraction
Raw client documents (PDFs, meeting transcripts, spreadsheets) are ingested into isolated
<untrusted_client_data> tags. The model operates under zero-shot instructions with negative
constraints: "Extract only stated operational metrics, financial values, and stated stakeholder claims
into JSON. Never impute missing metrics; if a value is not directly stated, record as null." Zero-shot is
mandatory here to prevent few-shot demonstration figures from bleeding into client records.

Stage 2: Strategic Reasoning & Scenario Deliberation
The extracted JSON feed is passed to the strategic reasoning engine. Here, Few-Shot Domain
Calibration is used: the prompt provides 2 exemplary case analyses demonstrating balanced
strategic evaluations across competing hypotheses (e.g., market expansion vs. margin defense).
Reasoning is explicitly controlled: open-ended narrative generation is blocked; the engine must
populate structured hypothesis trees, scenario matrices, and explicit hurdle rates.

Stage 3: Adversarial Validation & Quality Sign-Off
Before any deliverable is finalized, an automated adversarial fact-checker compares every claim in
the generated brief against the original extracted data from Stage 1. Any statement lacking direct
evidence is tagged as UNSUBSTANTIATED SPECULATION and routed to a human consultant for
manual review.

CORE IMPLEMENTATION RULES:

When to use Few-Shot vs. Zero-Shot:
  Zero-Shot: Use for data extraction, mathematical parsing, security checks, and tasks where the input
  must be the sole source of truth.

  Few-Shot: Use for complex classification, tone calibration, and teaching the model specific
  organizational frameworks that are difficult to explain with abstract rules alone.

When to enforce Reasoning vs. Suppress it:
  Enforce Reasoning: Use internally during diagnostic stages, anomaly detection, financial modeling,
  and scenario evaluations where intermediate steps prevent calculation errors.
  
  Suppress Reasoning: Suppress during final deliverable generation, executive briefing, programmatic
  JSON APIs, and customer-facing interfaces to keep outputs clear and focused.
 
Systematic Anti-Hallucination Guardrails:
  Explicit Delimited Sandboxing: Enclose all external text inside explicit XML tags (<context>...
  </context>).

  Mandatory Fallback Triggers: Require the model to output "NOT FOUND" or "DATA MISSING" instead of
  estimating unknown values.

  Multi-Stage Verification Pipelines: Decouple extraction from strategic analysis, running an automated
  check on generated claims before finalizing deliverables
